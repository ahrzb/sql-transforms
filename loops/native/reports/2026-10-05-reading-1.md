## 0. What this is {#what-this-is}

This is the native-transform catalog's first milestone report. It was taken
on master `5513891` on 2026-10-05, plus this report's own measuring tools,
which change nothing the catalog serves:
- `benchmarks/bench_native.py`;
- `coverage --modules`;
- the `NATIVE_SEEDS` variable.

There is no previous report. Deltas are against the loop's start that
morning, when only `StandardScaler` was native (1 of 68).

The milestone covers three things:
- six families: scalers, imputers, selectors, `PolynomialFeatures`,
  encoders and `KBinsDiscretizer`;
- a whole sklearn module native: `sklearn.feature_selection`;
- eleven catalog needs served by confit, in nine PRs.

An earlier attempt at this reading, on `b926e88`, found two confit defects.
confit fixed both within the hour (#350, #353), so the reading was re-taken.
Every number below was measured on `5513891` with confit's release build,
using the commands in [section 8](#environment-and-repro).

## 1. What moved {#what-moved}

**More than a third of sklearn's in-scope transformers serve natively, all
bit-exact.**
- 24 of 68 are native, up from 1 that morning: six scalers, two imputers,
  eleven selectors, `PolynomialFeatures`, three encoders and
  `KBinsDiscretizer`.
- Every entry is registered at 0 ulps and is held there by the
  swap-the-entry check.
- Over 200 seeds of every fixture configuration, 13,933 steps
  matched their twins bit for bit, with no breach ([section 4](#exactness)).

**A native entry serves a 64-row call 2x to 406x faster than its Python
twin.** This is measured on each class's widest fixture step.
- The widest steps are up to 276 lanes wide.
- A native call takes 29 to 7,164 µs. The twin takes 8,200 to 229,745 µs
  for the same call.
- The twin pays sklearn's input validation on every row. A selector or an
  encoder does little else, so those gain the most: 106x to 406x for
  selectors, and 47x to 351x for encoders.

**`sklearn.feature_selection` is entirely native.** All eleven selectors
inherit `SelectorMixin.transform` without overriding it. One translator
therefore serves them all: the kept columns, unchanged, taken from the
fitted mask.

**Categorical features serve natively.** `OrdinalEncoder`, `OneHotEncoder`
(dense) and `TargetEncoder` translate by asking the fitted twin for each
class's output: each fitted category, missing, and unknown. Infrequent
categories, `drop`, `handle_unknown` and target means are therefore the
twin's own values. Every encoder fixture includes string features with
unseen values and NULLs.

**confit served eleven catalog needs in one day, in nine PRs.** Each came
with a repro from the catalog ([section 6](#needs-from-confit)).
- **Wide steps build in seconds.**
  - A 128-feature `StandardScaler` with three groups used to hit the token
    cap. It now builds in 0.18 s.
  - `PolynomialFeatures` at 2,556 lanes was refused until #348. After #348
    it built in 287 s on this machine; after #350 it builds in 3.2 s.
- **The leak in long-lived processes is fixed.** Each build used to leak
  two memory mappings, so a process stalled at Linux's limit after about
  32,000 builds. This reading's first sweep hit that limit. #353 frees the
  memory: 6,000 builds now hold 477 mappings.

**One confit limit still caps the catalog: no shared subexpression.**
Every `Normalizer` lane repeats the row norm, because confit cannot
evaluate a repeated subexpression once per call.
- Its native call is only 2x faster than the twin.
- The max norm is capped at 8 features.
- l1 and l2 are refused at 32 features, and the refusal takes 6.9 s and
  16.7 s to arrive.

The confit loop has workers on both the shared subexpression (its ticket
T1) and the early refusal (T2).

## 4. Exactness {#exactness}

**All bit-exact.** Every registered bound is 0 ulps, across all 24 classes.

**The reading.** It covers 200 seeds for every fixture configuration, 70
configurations in all, in one pytest run on 4 workers (16 min).
- 13,933 steps were each checked row by row against their twin,
  and against their own definition run on DuckDB.
- There were 0 breaches.
- 67 steps stayed Python by a named cap: 61 `Normalizer(norm="max")` steps past the 8-feature cap, and 6 `Normalizer` l1 or l2 steps past confit's compiled-size limit.

**The first attempt.** Run on `b926e88`, its workers stalled at Linux's
limit on memory mappings past 61% of the run (finding 19). Run one class
per process to stay under that limit, it passed 11,933 of 12,000 steps
with the same 67 skips. This reading ran it again in one process after
#353.

**The order helpers.** `_helpers_test.py` holds four of numpy's
operations bit for bit at widths 1 to 300: the pairwise sum, the einsum
kernel, `max` and `clip`. A wrong order fails 40 to 50% of rows at every
width.

## 5. Findings {#findings}

These were in the catalog and its tests:

| # | finding | what it was | how it was found | fixed in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `finding: clip-nan-guard` | DuckDB orders NaN above every number, so `x > hi` is true for NaN where numpy's comparison is false. sklearn's array-API clip therefore needs a NaN guard to keep NaN. | the order helpers, DuckDB against numpy | #329 |
| 2 | `finding: l2-norm-kernel-order` | `row_norms` sums its squares in the order of numpy's einsum kernel, not left to right. The x86-64 baseline kernel uses two lanes and an unfused multiply-add. A platform whose kernel differs is detected by a probe and left Python. | a wrong order failed 40 to 50% of rows at every width | #329 |
| 3 | `finding: fixture-int-rounding-marker` | The fixture generator rounded an integer column after scrubbing the imputer's missing-value marker, so rounding could bring the marker back. | building the imputer fixtures | #332 |
| 4 | `finding: blas-oversubscription` | Every xdist worker ran a multi-threaded BLAS. An `RFECV` fixture took 35 s instead of 0.4 s. | timing the selector fixtures | #340 |
| 5 | `finding: matvec-cancellation` | PCA scores `x·c - m·c`. Near the mean the two terms cancel, so no small ulp bound exists for any summation order but BLAS's own. Over 200 seeds the error reached 9.9e9 ulps, yet stayed within 2.99 eps of the term scale. | measuring a left-to-right PCA entry | open: `decisions/open/matvec-parity-bound.md` |
| 6 | `finding: duckdb-reference-per-lane` | The DuckDB reference in `check()` read a wide struct field by field, and DuckDB expands the macro at every read. A 285-lane struct took 18 s. | profiling slow polynomial fixtures | #343 (one call per row: 0.1 s, same answers) |
| 7 | `finding: encoder-all-missing-string` | A string feature that is missing in every fit row has categories `[None]`, which the encoder read as numeric. `OrdinalEncoder(use_encoded_value)` and `OneHotEncoder(ignore)` gave wrong answers. | a targeted test while reviewing the encoders | #347 (translators get the declared types) |
| 8 | `finding: fixture-shapes-shared` | Every configuration of a class drew the same 8 data shapes, so the column behind finding 7 never appeared. | asking why the fixtures missed finding 7 | #347 (each configuration seeds its own) |
| 9 | `finding: onehot-sparse-unservable` | The default `OneHotEncoder()`, with sparse output, cannot be served by the Python step either: the step calls `float()` on a sparse row. | translating the encoders | open: `decisions/open/sparse-outputs.md` |

## 6. Needs from confit {#needs-from-confit}

**Delivered.** These are rows 10 to 20 above. What they unblocked:
- *#336:* serving a wide step without paying for every lane. Today a
  27-lane `StandardScaler` call takes 273 µs.
- *#338, #339, #348, #350:* wide steps build at all, and fast. A
  128-feature, three-group `StandardScaler` builds in 0.18 s, and a
  2,556-lane `PolynomialFeatures` in 3.2 s.
- *#341:* integer features build at the speed of DOUBLE ones, and deeply
  nested CASEs parse.
- *#346:* a named, catchable refusal where Cranelift corrupted a function.
- *#353:* a long-lived process can build without limit: a serving process
  that rebuilds, and the catalog's sweeps.
- *#337:* not used by the catalog yet. The max norm keeps its tournament
  of two-way CASEs.

**Open.**
- **A subexpression shared within one call.** This is the confit loop's
  ticket T1, in progress.
  - Waiting on it: `Normalizer` l1 and l2 past 16 features, the max norm
    past 8, and a `Normalizer` serve that is only 2x faster than the twin.
  - Compositions (a `Pipeline` of entries) will need it most, since each
    stage's lanes repeat inside the next stage's lanes.
- **An early size refusal.** This is the confit loop's ticket T2, in
  progress. A refusal arrives only after Cranelift has spent its time: up
  to 47 s for a 48-feature l2 `Normalizer`. `to_native` pays that before
  falling back to Python.

**Sent since this reading,** from wave 1's reviews:
- A DOUBLE negation `-x` builds and serves far slower than `-1.0 * x`,
  which is the same double: 32 ms against 1.1 ms per 64-row call (the
  `QuantileTransformer` worker).
- A guarded `ln` or `exp` counts as able to trap, so every field read
  evaluates every lane's calls. Box-Cox serving grows with the square of
  the width, and the entry stops at 12 features (the `PowerTransformer`
  worker).
