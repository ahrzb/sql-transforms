## 1. What moved {#what-moved}

**Four linear projections serve within the matvec parity bound, and the
coverage is 35 of 68.**
- T37 (#458) serves `PCA`, `IncrementalPCA`, `TruncatedSVD` and
  `GaussianRandomProjection`. Each multiplies the input row by a fitted
  matrix. Before T37, these four classes stayed Python.
- The twin hands this product to BLAS, numpy's linear algebra library.
  BLAS adds the terms in an order that depends on the CPU, and SQL cannot
  follow that order. So the entry cannot be bit-exact.
- Each output field of the entry is within (n + 3)·eps·S + τ of the twin.
  Here n is the number of features, and S is the error scale of the field.
  This is the bound that the owner ruled
  ([matvec-parity-bound.md](../decisions/closed/matvec-parity-bound.md)).
  The largest measured distance is 3.90·eps·S, for `TruncatedSVD` at 17
  features, where K = n + 3 is 20 ([section 4](#exactness)).
- These entries serve only with `to_native(step, allow_bound=True)`.
  Inside a `Pipeline`, `ColumnTransformer` or `FeatureUnion`, a projection
  stays Python ([Needs the owner](#needs-the-owner)).
- Over few features, the entry is fast. `PCA` over 16 features of 4
  components serves a row in 1.4 µs, against the twin's 140 µs. Over many
  features, it is slow. At 256 features of 64 components, it serves a row
  in 1,126 µs, against the twin's 403 µs. Each read of a feature costs
  confit about 60 ns (finding 73). Confit #459, merged after this
  reading's measurements, addresses this cost
  ([section 6](#needs-from-confit)).

**The sparse configurations serve.**
- T36 (#455) serves a step whose `transform` returns a scipy sparse
  matrix. The defaults of `OneHotEncoder`, `KBinsDiscretizer` and
  `RandomTreesEmbedding` return one. So do `MissingIndicator(sparse=True)`
  and `SplineTransformer(sparse_output=True)`.
- The twin now reads a sparse output as its dense array, as the owner
  ruled ([sparse-outputs.md](../decisions/closed/sparse-outputs.md)).
  Before T36, the twin could not serve these configurations (finding 9),
  and they stayed Python.
- A `Pipeline`, `ColumnTransformer` or `FeatureUnion` with a sparse output
  serves too, with the limits of findings 70 and 71.
- The coverage does not change, because each of these classes already
  had an entry.
- Three classes have a new widest step, each a sparse configuration. The
  sparse `SplineTransformer` over 22 features serves a call of 64 rows in
  2.7 ms, against the twin's 351 ms.

**Since T36's commit, confit builds a wide step up to 7 times as slowly
(finding 76).**
- Confit #456 merged just before T36. Since #456, the build of a struct
  read field by field grows about as the square of the fields read. The
  catalog's query reads each output field in this way.
- `RandomTreesEmbedding` of 100 trees of depth 6 builds in 18.1 to 18.3 s,
  against 3.7 s at reading 5. As a struct read field by field, 250 trees
  of depth 5 build in 42 to 44 s, against 5.9 to 6.5 s.
- The serve times did not change. A step with few output fields builds as
  before.
- The families' build estimates were fitted before #456. So now a step
  within an estimated 7 s can build for 18 s or more.
- The native loop sent the confit loop a reproduction that uses confit
  alone. The confit loop takes this need next
  ([section 6](#needs-from-confit)).

**The widest `Pipeline` builds about 3 times as fast as at reading 5
(T35, finding 69).**
- T35 (#454) spells the input guard as one test for each kind of rejected
  value. Each test is a `greatest` or a `least` over the features that
  reject the value. Before T35, the guard had one test for each feature,
  joined by OR.
- In the bench, in the dev shell, confit builds the widest `Pipeline`
  step in 0.90 to 1.00 s, against 2.75 to 3.15 s at reading 5. The step
  serves a call of 64 rows in 5.2 to 6.0 ms, against 6.2 to 9.9 ms
  ([section 3](#speed)).
- The native bench now fails where a step serves more slowly than its
  twin.
- Reading 5 said that this step served more slowly than its twin from T25
  (#423), which added the input guard, to T28 (#437). That was wrong.
  Reading 5's search measured these commits with reading 4's confit
  (finding 74). With each commit's own confit, the step served a call in
  5.6 to 5.9 ms there, and its twin takes about 85 ms.

**The wide encoders build in about a second, not in tens of seconds (T31
and T33).**
- T31 (#447): an encoder probes its categories with about four calls of
  `transform` for each feature, not one call for each category (finding
  65). `to_native` of `OrdinalEncoder` over 32 string features of 125
  categories takes 1.4 s. At reading 5, it took 40.8 s first in the
  process.
- T33 (#450): the encoders' tests find a number in a run of consecutive
  integer categories by a range. `to_native` of `OrdinalEncoder` over 32
  number features of 125 integer categories takes 1.3 to 1.4 s.

**`QuantileTransformer` and `IsotonicRegression` stay Python past an
estimated 7 s build (T34, finding 68).**
- T34 (#452) gives them the cap of the forests and the splines. Before
  T34, the cap was a count of 8,000 quantiles or thresholds.
- `QuantileTransformer` at 8,000 quantiles over one feature still serves.
  `to_native` takes 8.7 to 9.0 s. T34 measured 1.7 to 1.9 s of this time
  in confit's pass over the function body (finding 72).
- 10 quantiles over 800 features now stay Python, at an estimated 11 s.
  With the cap lifted, `to_native` takes 63 s first in the process and
  60 s after it, on master (finding 76).
- `IsotonicRegression` serves 11,000 thresholds (`to_native` takes 7.2
  to 7.3 s) and stays Python at 12,000.

**Two kinds of step went back to Python.**
- Before T30 (#446), a forest had two spellings. The catalog used one
  CASE per tree if its estimated build was within 7 s. Else it used the
  paths, if their estimated build was within 7 s. The paths spell each
  leaf as the conjunction of the branch tests on its path.
- At 8 features, the paths served forests of about 5,990 to 6,140 output
  fields. T30 made one CASE per tree the only spelling, so these forests
  now stay Python.
- 10 quantiles over 800 features stay Python since T34 (above).

**The error scales.** The four linear projections (`project.py`) declare
one error scale. For output field k of a row x, with n features:

- S = (Σ_i |x_i·c_ki| + Σ_i |m_i·c_ki|) / s_k. Here c_ki is the
  coefficient of feature i in component k, and m_i is the mean of feature
  i (0 for `TruncatedSVD` and `GaussianRandomProjection`). s_k is the
  whitening scale of component k under `whiten=True`, else 1.
- K = n + 3. The docstring of `project.py` derives K from the rounding
  error of each operation, as the ruling asks
  ([matvec-parity-bound.md](../decisions/closed/matvec-parity-bound.md)).
- τ = (2n/s_k + 1)·2^-1074, for products too small for a double. 2^-1074
  is the smallest positive double.

The reason for the bound is the order of the twin's additions. The twin
hands the product to BLAS, numpy's linear algebra library, which adds the
terms in an order that depends on the CPU. SQL cannot follow that order.
The entry adds the products in pairs, then the pair sums in pairs, and so
on.
