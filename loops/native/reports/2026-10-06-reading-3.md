## 0. What this is {#what-this-is}

This is the native catalog's third milestone report, at the end of wave 3.
It was taken on master `87636ef` over the night of 2026-10-05 to 06, with
confit's release build. The bench, the width curves and the sweep ran on
`81c3344`; the one commit between, #389, is an owner record and changes no

The commands are reading 2's, in [section 8](#environment-and-repro). One
tooling change comes with this report. `bench_native` now draws each row
inside the fitted range where a transform raises outside it, and draws

## 1. What moved {#what-moved}

**`ColumnTransformer` and `FeatureUnion` serve natively, bit-exact, as
`Pipeline` does.**
- `ColumnTransformer` and `FeatureUnion` translate to the concatenation of
  their parts' translations (#382), as `Pipeline` composes its steps'.
  Each part is translated over its own columns, using sklearn's own column
  selection run on the column indices. A transformer weight is the twin's
  product in doubles.
- The shape most often deployed is a `ColumnTransformer` routing an
  encoder over the string columns and a scaling pipeline over the numeric
  ones. It is a fixture now, and it is bit-exact.
- The widest fixture of each:
  - `ColumnTransformer`, 24 features into 103 lanes: 64 rows in 1,130 µs
    against the twin's 54,407, 48x;
  - `FeatureUnion`, 32 features into 650 lanes: 9,779 µs against 355,903,
    36x.
- A composition serves only bit-exact parts. Any other part is refused,
  and the refusal names it.
- coverage.md now says so on each composition row, and gives `Pipeline` a
  row of its own (#385). sklearn's transformer list leaves `Pipeline` out.

**30 of 68 in-scope transformers are native, up from 27, and
`sklearn.preprocessing` is complete.**
- `SplineTransformer` (#384) was the last "not yet" row there. All 15
  in-scope preprocessing transformers are now native.
- `IsotonicRegression` (#381) completes `sklearn.isotonic`.
- `FeatureAgglomeration` (#380) is the first native row of
  `sklearn.cluster`.
- All three are bit-exact. Each new family's twin was read in its own
  library's source, with numpy's `bincount`, scipy's `interp1d` and
  scipy's compiled de Boor recurrence followed operation by operation
  ([section 5](#findings), findings 37 to 39).

**The multi-instance `QuantileTransformer` serves 28x faster than its twin
again.**
- Reading 2's one regression was confit #363 evaluating shared
  subexpressions inside CASE arms a row never takes. confit #377 fixed it.
- The widest fixture, three instances over 27 features, now serves 64
  rows in 1,003 µs against the twin's 28,318. At reading 2 it took
  21,001 µs, 1.4x.
- Its build fell from 2.98 s to 1.06 s.

**The max norm and `sin`/`cos` serve at any width.**
- #378 adopted confit #374, which lowers `greatest` as a running extreme,
  and #375, under which a guarded `sin`/`cos` cannot trap. That lifted
  `Normalizer`'s max-norm cap (48 features) and `FunctionTransformer`'s
  `sin`/`cos` cap (8 features).
- In this reading's sweep neither has a skip. Reading 2 had 96 `sin`/`cos`
  skips.

**Splines build 4x faster since confit #387, and their cap is now too
strict.**
- On one container, alternating confit before and after #387 three times:
  a 16-feature, degree-3, 8-knot `continue` spline builds in 4.4–4.6 s
  before and 0.98–1.08 s after.
- The spline entry's build estimate was fitted before #387. It refuses
  steps that now build in 1.1 to 4.1 s, for example 32 features at degree
  3 with 8 knots: estimated 22 s, actual 2.3 s.
- Re-fitting it is ticketed as T13.
- `QuantileTransformer`'s builds did not change across #387, within noise.
