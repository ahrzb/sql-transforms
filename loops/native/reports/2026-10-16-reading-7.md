# Native catalog, reading 7 (2026-10-16)

## 0. What this is {#what-this-is}

This is the native catalog's seventh milestone report. It is also a
reading: it measures each success measure on one master commit,
`f54ddab`, which merged on 2026-10-16 at 04:58 UTC. The previous report is
the [short reading](2026-10-15-short-reading.md) of 2026-10-15, on the
master commit `fbbcd24`. The short reading did not measure speed. So
[section 3](#speed) compares the speed with
[reading 6](2026-10-15-reading-6.md), on the master commit `98033e8`.
T38 to T41 are the loop's tickets ([`tickets.md`](../tickets.md)), and a
number such as #494 is a PR.

The trigger of this report is "KPI crosses a mark"
([`reporting.md`](../../reporting.md)). For the native loop, one such mark
is a whole sklearn module that goes native, and T41 (#494) made two
modules wholly native. T41 serves all four classes of
`sklearn.cross_decomposition`, the PLS classes: `PLSRegression`,
`PLSCanonical`, `CCA` and `PLSSVD`. It also serves
`LinearDiscriminantAnalysis`, which is the one class of
`sklearn.discriminant_analysis` in scope, and `FastICA`. The native
coverage went from 35 to 41 of the 68 sklearn classes in scope. A class is
in scope where the catalog contract does not leave it out
([section 2](#scoreboard)).

**Terms of this report.** A *step* is one fitted sklearn transformer. The
catalog's tests draw their steps from fixture configurations
(`catalog_test.FIXTURES`). A *configuration* is one set of a class's
parameters. A seed selects one step of a configuration: the configuration
fitted on random data that the seed sets. `to_native` is the catalog's
function that translates a step into its catalog entry. Where `to_native`
raises `NotNative`, the step *stays Python*: its twin serves it.
`to_native` builds the entry's query once with confit, to check that
confit accepts it: this is the *trial build*. A *native* call is a call
that the entry serves. The *width* of a step is its number of output
fields. A script name such as `pca_at.py` names a script of this reading,
in the project's shared folder ([section 8](#environment-and-repro)).

**The machine.** Every measurement ran on a machine of the type of reading
6, with confit's release build and the commands of reading 6
([section 8](#environment-and-repro)). This machine started on 2026-10-16
at 03:48 UTC, after reading 6, so it may run on another host. So a delta
against reading 6 measures the code, the noise between runs, and the
change of machine. [Section 3](#speed) measures reading 6's commit again
on this machine, to tell the machine from the code. This machine is a
little slower than reading 6's machine. The measurements use two Pythons:
the CPython of the dev shell, which is the repository's Nix environment,
and uv's CPython, as reading 6 did ([section 8](#environment-and-repro)).

## 1. What moved {#what-moved}

**Six more classes serve within an error scale, and the coverage is 41 of
68.**
- T41 (#494) serves `FastICA`, `LinearDiscriminantAnalysis`,
  `PLSRegression`, `PLSCanonical`, `CCA` and `PLSSVD`. Before T41, a step
  of these six classes stayed Python. `sklearn.cross_decomposition` and
  `sklearn.discriminant_analysis` are now wholly native
  ([section 2](#scoreboard)).
- These are the centered projections. The twin subtracts a fitted mean
  from the row, and may divide each difference by a fitted scale. Then it
  multiplies the result by a fitted matrix. The entry does the subtraction
  and the division as the twin does. So the entry and the twin differ only
  in the order in which they add the products.
- Each output field of the entry is within K·eps·S + τ of the twin, with
  K = n + 1, where n is the number of features ([section 4](#exactness)).
  `PCA` subtracts its projected mean and divides by its whitening scale
  after the sum. These two operations add 2 to K, so K is n + 3 for
  `PCA`. The largest measured distance is 4.09·eps·S, for `PLSCanonical`
  at 64 features, where K is 65.
- These entries serve only if the caller asks for a bound:
  `to_native(step, allow_bound=True)`.
- At their widest steps of the bench, the six classes serve a call of 64
  rows in 77 to 202 µs, 49 to 107 times as fast as their twins
  ([section 3](#speed)).
- The parity run compares each entry with its twin over 200 seeds of each
  configuration ([section 4](#exactness)). 236 of its 800 `FastICA` steps
  stay Python. In 133 of them, the fit broke down (finding 78). The other
  103 whiten a component of rounding noise, as a whitened `PCA` can. The
  ruling on the bound does not serve such a step.

**Confit #459 and #461 made the native calls and the builds faster.**
- Both merged on 2026-10-15, after reading 6's measurements, each for a
  need of the catalog ([section 6](#needs-from-confit)). Confit #459
  compiles a small CASE that cannot trap without a branch. It does the
  same for the NULL guard of each field read. Confit #461 checks one time
  for each SQL function call whether the call holds a subquery, not again
  at each field read.
- The search over the commits since reading 6 timed five widest steps of
  the bench at each of these confit changes ([section 3](#speed)). With
  #459, the widest `PolynomialFeatures` serves a call in 1.8 to 1.9 ms,
  against 3.7 to 4.4 ms before, and it builds in 0.03 s, against 0.16 to
  0.27 s. With #461, the widest `RandomTreesEmbedding` builds in 0.15 to
  0.18 s, against 0.46 to 0.65 s with #459 alone.
- In the bench, a native call takes a median of 0.90 times reading 6's
  time, and 17 classes build faster in every run. The widest `Pipeline`
  builds in 0.47 to 0.57 s, against 0.90 to 1.00 s in reading 6's bench.
  So the KPI `widest_build_s` is 0.49 s, against 0.93 s.
- This machine is a little slower than reading 6's machine. The bench
  of reading 6's commit, run on this machine, serves a native call in a
  median of 1.04 times reading 6's time, and a twin call in 1.11 times.
  On this machine, master serves a native call in a median of 0.85 times
  the time of reading 6's commit ([section 3](#speed)).

**`PCA` over many features now serves faster than its twin (finding 73,
fixed by confit #459).**
- At 256 features of 64 components, `PCA` serves a row in 39.6 µs, 12
  times as fast as its twin. At reading 6, it served a row in 1,126 µs,
  2.8 times as long as its twin.
- From 16 to 256 features, `PCA` now serves 12 to 163 times as fast as
  its twin. The centered projections serve a row at 256 features of 64
  components in 18.5 to 19.7 µs, 22 to 27 times as fast as their twins,
  and about twice as fast as `PCA` (finding 85, below).

**Wide steps build several times as fast as at reading 6 (finding 76,
fixed by confit #461).**
- `RandomTreesEmbedding` of 100 trees of depth 6 builds in 2.33 s,
  against 18.29 s at reading 6 and 3.70 s at reading 5.
- As a struct read field by field, 250 trees of depth 5 build in 2.99 to
  3.24 s, against 41.9 to 44.4 s at reading 6 and 5.9 to 6.5 s at
  reading 5.
- A family whose builds can take long estimates the build time of each
  step from its size. Past an estimated 7 s, the step stays Python: this
  is the cap. T39 and T40 measured builds on the faster confit and fitted
  the coefficients of these estimates to them again. So the caps take
  more steps. `IsotonicRegression` of 12,000 thresholds now serves, and so
  does `PCA` over 256 features of 256 components. `QuantileTransformer`
  over 800 features of 10 quantiles still stays Python, at an estimated
  7.1 s, against 11 s at reading 6.

**On this machine, a build that a cap takes can take up to about 9 s.**
- When T39, T40 and T41 fitted their estimates, the builds of 1 s or more
  took 0.79 to 1.31 times the estimate. On this machine, they take 1.02 to
  1.51 times the estimate, and the slowest build that a cap takes is
  9.2 s ([section 3](#speed)).
- T39 and T40 fitted their estimates on reading 6's machine. Here, T40's
  builds take 1.05 to 1.36 times as long as T40 measured them. At the
  commits of T38, confit #464 and T40, the same builds take as long as at
  master. So the change of machine, not the code, makes them slower.

**Bad news.**
- In 54 output fields of the parity run, `FastICA` at one feature answers
  0.0, where its twin answers −0.0 (finding 81). The bound allows either
  sign of a zero. But each entry gives the twin's sign where it can, and
  T38 did this for the four linear projections of T37 (`PCA`,
  `IncrementalPCA`, `TruncatedSVD` and `GaussianRandomProjection`). The
  next ticket, T43, gives these fields the twin's sign too.
- The fixture configuration of `TruncatedSVD` with ARPACK has no
  `random_state` (finding 82). So the same seed can give a different step
  in each process, against the rule that a seed always gives the same
  step. The parity run cannot repeat these steps. T43 gives this
  configuration a seed.
- Reading 6's table of ulp bounds gave, by mistake, reading 5's numbers
  of the rows measured (finding 83). This report gives the correct
  numbers ([section 4](#exactness)).
- `PCA` over many features serves at about twice the time of the
  centered projections (finding 85). A shared value is a value that the
  SQL function builds once and reads many times, such as a feature's
  `coalesce(x, NaN)`. The input guard of the first output field reads
  each feature's shared value, and the sums of `PCA` read the same shared
  values. With confit alone, such a guard doubles the time of a call. The
  second item of the section "Next" of the loop's PLANS tries to avoid
  this in the catalog ([section 7](#left-open)).
- To build the input guard, `to_native` calls the twin on rows of special
  values, to find the values that the twin rejects: this is the probe.
  For a `QuantileTransformer` over 800 features, `to_native` spends 8.0
  to 9.1 s of its 19.7 to 20.4 s in the probe (finding 84). The cap
  estimates only the trial build, which takes 9.4 to 10.9 s here. This
  step stays Python, because its estimated build is above 7 s.

**The error scales.** The family of the projections (`project.py`)
declares two error scales. For output field k of a row x, with n
features:

- The four linear projections of T37 (`project.py`): S = (Σ_i |x_i·c_ki|
  + Σ_i |m_i·c_ki|) / s_k, K = n + 3 and τ = (2n/s_k + 1)·2^-1074, as at
  reading 6. Here c_ki is the coefficient of feature i in component k.
  m_i is the mean of feature i, which is 0 for `TruncatedSVD` and
  `GaussianRandomProjection`. s_k is the whitening scale of component k
  under `whiten=True`, else 1. 2^-1074 is the smallest positive double.
- The six centered projections of T41 (`_project_centered` in
  `project.py`): S = Σ_i |d_i·c_ki|, K = n + 1 and τ = 2n·2^-1074. The
  twin first subtracts a fitted mean μ_i from each feature x_i. The PLS
  classes (the four classes of `sklearn.cross_decomposition`) then divide
  each difference by a fitted scale σ_i. d_i is the
  value that results for feature i. The table below gives d_i and c_ki
  for each class.

| class | d_i | c_ki, the coefficient of feature i for output field k |
|---|---|---|
| `FastICA` | x_i − μ_i under `whiten`, with μ = `mean_`; else x_i | row k of `components_` |
| `LinearDiscriminantAnalysis` | x_i − μ_i under the solver "svd", with μ = `xbar_`; else x_i | column k of `scalings_` |
| `PLSRegression`, `PLSCanonical`, `CCA` | (x_i − μ_i) / σ_i, with μ = `_x_mean` and σ = `_x_std` | column k of `x_rotations_` |
| `PLSSVD` | the same | column k of `x_weights_` |

The entry computes each d_i with the same subtraction and division as the
twin, so its d_i is equal to the twin's. Only the order of the sums
differs. `PCA` subtracts its projected mean and divides by its whitening
scale after the sum, and each of these two operations adds 1 to K. The
centered projections subtract and divide before the sum, as their twins
do. So K is n + 1 here, against n + 3 for `PCA`.

The reason for both bounds is the order of the twin's additions. The twin
hands the product to BLAS, numpy's linear algebra library, which adds the
terms in an order that depends on the CPU. SQL cannot follow that order.
The entry adds the products in pairs, then the pair sums in pairs, and so
on. The docstrings of `project.py` derive each K from the rounding error
of each operation, as the ruling asks
([matvec-parity-bound.md](../decisions/closed/matvec-parity-bound.md)).

## 6. Needs from confit {#needs-from-confit}

A need is a change in confit that the catalog asks the confit loop for.
Each need has a short name in backticks, which the KPI block lists under
`needs_from_other_loop` while the need is open.

**Delivered since reading 6:** needs 1 and 2 of reading 6, by confit #461
and #459. Both merged on 2026-10-15, after reading 6's measurements. This
reading measured them on `f54ddab` with the reproductions that the native
loop sent to the confit loop. Each reproduction uses confit alone, without
the catalog's code.

1. **A struct read field by field that builds about as fast as before
   confit #456** (`field-reads-quadratic`, finding 76). Confit #461 checks
   one time for each SQL function call whether the call holds a subquery,
   not again at each field read. A struct of 4,000 fields, read field by
   field, now builds in 0.66 to 0.74 s. At reading 6, it built in 5.84 to
   6.13 s, and before #456 in 0.92 to 1.01 s. The build grows about as the
   number of fields: 0.06 to 0.09 s at 500 fields, 0.15 to 0.16 s at
   1,000, 0.28 to 0.29 s at 2,000 and 0.66 to 0.74 s at 4,000. Read whole,
   the struct of 4,000 fields builds in 0.33 to 0.39 s, against 0.60 to
   0.67 s at reading 6.
2. **Reads of a shared value as fast as reads of a parameter**
   (`shared-value-reads`, finding 73). A parameter is an input of the SQL
   function, which the body reads directly. A shared value is a value
   that the body builds once and reads many times, such as a feature's
   `coalesce(x, NaN)`. Confit #459 compiles a small CASE that cannot trap
   without a branch. It does the same for the NULL guard of each field
   read. In the reproduction, 256 features, each read by 16 sums of
   products, serve 64 rows in 2.4 to 2.9 ms over the shared values
   `coalesce(x, NaN)`, in three runs. Over the parameters, they serve in
   1.7 to 1.9 ms. At reading 6, the two took 17.2 to 18.5 ms and 1.7 ms.
   A call reads 4,096 shared values for each of its 64 rows. So a read of
   a shared value now costs about 2 to 5 ns more than a read of a
   parameter, against about 60 ns at reading 6. Over the shared values,
   the function builds in 0.22 to 0.28 s, against 0.81 to 1.26 s at
   reading 6. Over the parameters, it builds in 0.20 to 0.27 s, against
   0.26 s. The catalog's `PCA` over 256 features of 64 components serves a
   row in 39.6 µs, against 1,125.8 µs at reading 6
   ([section 3](#speed)).

**Open:**

3. **A struct output read field by field, built once for the call**
   (`struct-field-reads`, the rest of finding 60). Confit builds each field
   read of a struct output on its own. On `f54ddab`, 100 trees of depth 5
   build in 0.89 to 1.10 s as a struct read field by field, and in 0.51 to
   0.57 s as a list. 250 trees of depth 5 build in 2.99 to 3.24 s against
   1.33 to 1.44 s. So the struct takes 1.6 to 2.4 times the time of the
   list, against 9 to 22 times at reading 6 ([section 3](#speed)). This

   7 s and it stays Python (the cap, [section 3](#speed)).
4. **A named refusal, not a Cranelift verifier error**
   (`verifier-error-refusal`, finding 63). Cranelift is confit's code

   (`confit-wide-encoder-verifier.py`) builds, as at reading 6:
   `to_native` translates the step, and the whole run takes 4.8 s. No
   step that this reading measured meets the error.
5. **A cheaper pass over a function body in Python**
   (`function-body-pass`, finding 72). With confit alone, confit's pass

   leaves, the pass takes 0.31 s, against 0.29 s, and the build and one
   call take 1.16 s, against 1.70 s. Low priority.
