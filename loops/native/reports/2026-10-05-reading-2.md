# Native catalog, reading 2 (2026-10-05)

## 0. What this is {#what-this-is}

This is the native catalog's second milestone report, at the end of wave 2
(the loop's second wave of workers). It was taken on master `89e99fc` on
2026-10-05, with confit's release build. The previous report is
[reading 1](2026-10-05-reading-1.md), on master `5513891`, taken the same
morning.

The commands are reading 1's, in [section 8](#environment-and-repro). One
tooling change comes with this report: `bench_native` now draws strictly
positive rows for a fixture marked `positive`, so that the Box-Cox twin
answers them. Before, its `PowerTransformer` row raised. Nothing the
catalog serves changes.

Since reading 1, nine native PRs merged:
- four families: `QuantileTransformer` (#357), `PowerTransformer` (#356),
  `FunctionTransformer` (#367) and `Pipeline` (#368);
- two adoptions of confit capabilities: #369 (confit #362) and #372
  (confit #363 and #358);
- the loop's move to `loops/native/` (#366), its ticket board (#365), and
  reading 1 itself (#360).

## 1. What moved {#what-moved}

**Pipelines of catalog entries serve natively, bit-exact.** A fitted
`Pipeline` translates to the composition of its steps' translations (#368).
That covers nested pipelines, `"passthrough"` and `None` steps.
- Because every step is a 0-ulp entry, the composition is bit-exact by
  induction: each step hands the next exactly the twin's doubles, signed
  zeros included.
- The widest pipeline fixture, `KBinsDiscretizer` into a dense
  `OneHotEncoder` over 26 features and 324 lanes, serves 64 rows in
  2,022 µs against the twin's 69,693: 34x.
- Pipelines are what users deploy. A pipeline of the catalog's bit-exact
  entries now serves natively. It is refused, by name, if a step is not in
  the catalog or carries a bound, or if a step before the last returns
  something other than float64.
- sklearn's transformer list has no `Pipeline`, so the scoreboard below
  cannot show this (PLANS "Next", item 3).

**27 of 68 in-scope transformers are native, up from 24.** The three new
ones are all in `sklearn.preprocessing`, which goes from 11 of 19 to 14:
- `QuantileTransformer`: uniform output, bit-exact, with `np.interp`
  spelled in numpy's order;
- `PowerTransformer`: Box-Cox with `standardize=False`, within 4 ulps
  ([section 4](#exactness));
- `FunctionTransformer`: the identity and 15 numpy functions, bit-exact,
  each checked against numpy on signed zeros, subnormals, halves,
  infinities and NaN.

**A `Normalizer` serves at any width, and 34x faster than its twin, where
reading 1 had 2x.** confit now computes the norm every lane repeats once
per row (#363), and refuses an oversized query right after lowering (#358).
- l1 and l2 at 32 features were refused after 6.9 s and 16.7 s. They now
  build in 0.10 s and 0.23 s.
- At 128 features they build in 2.1 s and 5.4 s.
- The max norm goes from 8 features to 48, spelled as one `greatest`
  (#372).
- The widest `Normalizer` fixture serves 64 rows in 520 µs, against
  7,164 µs at reading 1.

**Box-Cox serves at any width.** confit #362 counts `exp` and a negation as
unable to trap, and `ln` too under a guard that rules out `x <= 0`. The entry
adds a dead guard arm that confit can read (#369). At 64 features a row now
serves in 50 µs against the twin's 329, where it took 3,448 µs before, and
the 12-feature cap is gone.

**One regression, and it is confit's: a multi-instance
`QuantileTransformer` serves only 1.4x faster than its twin, where it was
25x.**
- confit #363 evaluates every shared subexpression before the first item,
  including those that occur only inside CASE arms a row does not take.
- `QuantileTransformer`'s widest fixture has three instances whose
  per-feature search trees share subtrees. It serves 64 rows in 21,001 µs
  on master, and in 1,153 µs on the commit before #363. The twin takes
  28,699.
- One instance is unaffected; two are 2x slower.
- I sent it to the confit loop with a reproduction. It heads PLANS "Needs
  from confit" ([section 6](#needs-from-confit)).

**The default `QuantileTransformer()` now serves up to four features.**
sklearn's default is 1,000 quantiles. The cap now bounds both the sum of the
quantiles over the features (4,000) and the sum of their squares
(4,000,000), because a feature's build grows as 0.57 ms·q + 0.87 µs·q².
The slowest step served, 1,000 quantiles over four features, builds in
6.7 s. Reading 1's cap was 2,000 summed, so the default served two features.
